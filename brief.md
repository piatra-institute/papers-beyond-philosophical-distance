# Brief

## Question

Maps of philosophical positions are built from pairwise relations: distances between positions, correlations between survey questions, co-citation links. A synthesis of commitments can be pairwise compatible and jointly incompatible. When does k-wise compatibility of a family of commitments decide its joint compatibility, how large must k be for the commitment types an atlas actually uses, and do real position data carry higher-order structure that a pairwise map misses?

## Claim

The number of commitments an atlas must check at once is a property of its representation class: the largest minimal incompatible family that the class admits, which is the Helly number of the class. Pairwise maps decide joint compatibility exactly when this order is two. Categorical answers and their conjunctions have order two, so pairwise maps of answer profiles are complete. Order grows once commitments carry inferential links or geometric shape: implication rules and parity constraints reach n+1 on n binary issues, arbitrary pairwise logical links 2n, convex regions d+1 in a continuous d-dimensional space but 2^d on a grid of ordinal positions and at least six on a plotted map of thirty positions in general position. Mixing two classes of low order can produce high order. Commitment-level order and issue-level locality (consistency width, acyclic scopes) are different properties and must not be confused. Pairwise statistics of observed positions determine exactly their majority-closed hull, so parity-type structure is invisible to them. In public data, third-order structure is small but measurable in 71,299 lay judgments on three trolley cases, and undetectable at the sample size of a 307-person survey of consciousness researchers, whose power to detect it is quantified.

## Kind

Formal result with proofs, exhaustive finite verification, and a test on public data (`has_simulation: true`). Genre: theorem-and-measurement paper.

## Strongest serious alternative

The local-to-global facts are known in their home fields: Helly numbers in convex geometry, near-unanimity and local consistency in constraint satisfaction, acyclic schemes in databases, the median property and quota thresholds in judgment aggregation, contextuality in quantum foundations. On this view nothing is added by restating them for maps of philosophy. The paper answers by stating the translation precisely, proving the orders of the commitment classes a philosophical atlas uses (several of which, such as the mixture effect, the planar-map bound and the statistical hull, are not stated in those literatures for this purpose), separating the two notions of locality, and measuring the higher-order statistical structure in real position data.

A second alternative: maps of philosophy are exploratory aids, not inference engines, so their failure to decide joint compatibility is harmless. The answer is that such maps are used to propose syntheses and locate unoccupied combinations, and the order result says when that use is sound.
