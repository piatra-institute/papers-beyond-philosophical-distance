# Sources

Frozen bibliography for the manuscript (48 entries in `references.yaml`). Identities were checked on 15 November 2026 against Crossref and DataCite records (DOIs), the GDZ/EuDML records for the two German articles without DOIs, OpenLibrary and JSTOR for books, and the arXiv API for preprints. `papers refs --verify` reports identity matches for every DOI except Nielsen and Parsons (2007), whose Crossref record lacks a year (see below). Depth below means what was actually read: identity (record only), abstract, or full text, with the copy used. Page locators refer to the copy read, which is often a preprint or accepted manuscript; version-of-record pages were not compared unless stated.

## Transcription changes

- Helly (1923): the GDZ/EuDML metadata misprint the title as "...Punkte"; the printed title, used here, reads "Punkten". German titles carry `language: de`.
- Averkov (2013): the registry title writes $S$ in TeX math, kept as such.
- Malaterre, Chartier and Pulizzotto (2019): the journal title in the article title is set in italics, as in the registry.
- Bourget and Chalmers (2023): Crossref gives volume 23, issue 1 and no article number; author pages give article 11. Only the volume and DOI are entered.
- Shi and Haupt (2026): the FAccT '26 version of record is cited; the text read was arXiv 2604.23575v2.
- Foot: the 1967 Oxford Review article has no registry record; the Oxford University Press reprint (2002, DOI 10.1093/0199252866.003.0002) is cited with `original-date: 1967`.
- No arrows or other symbols outside the build fonts occur in titles. The en dashes in two titles are in Palatino.

## Helly numbers, convexity and empty polygons

- **helly1923**. Helly (1923), Jahresbericht der Deutschen Mathematiker-Vereinigung 32:175-176 (no DOI; GDZ PPN37721857X_0032). Read: page images. p. 175 states the theorem for closed convex regions in n dimensions, every n+1 of which meet. Use: order d+1 of convex regions.
- **danzer1963**. Danzer, Grünbaum and Klee (1963), in Convexity, Proc. Symp. Pure Math. 7, AMS, pp. 101-180, DOI 10.1090/pspum/007/0157289. Read: scan in Grünbaum's paper archive. p. 102: Helly's theorem; p. 124: the Helly number α, and Motzkin's result for algebraic varieties (degree one gives flats; not stated in words for flats); p. 128: notation γ_κ; p. 144, item (c): axis-parallel parallelotopes have γ₂ = 1. Use: definition of the Helly number, order two of boxes.
- **doignon1973**. Doignon (1973), J. Geometry 3(1):71-85, DOI 10.1007/BF01949705. Identity only (Springer challenge, no open copy). The result h(Z^d) = 2^d is used as stated by Averkov (2013, abstract and Remark 4.5) and Amenta, De Loera and Soberón (2017, arXiv p. 3).
- **averkov2013**. Averkov (2013), SIAM J. Discrete Math. 27(3):1610-1624, DOI 10.1137/110850463. Read: arXiv 1109.6179v3 (preprint). Definitions 1.3-1.4 (p. 2), eq. (3.1) (p. 5: a family and its finite intersections have equal Helly number), Proposition 3.1 (p. 6: h(S) <= |S|, h(S ∩ C) <= h(S)), Remark 4.5 (p. 11). Use: Proposition 2's credit, the grid bound, the occupancy characterization.
- **hoffman1979**. Hoffman (1979), Ann. N.Y. Acad. Sci. 319(1):284-288, DOI 10.1111/j.1749-6632.1979.tb32803.x. Identity only (closed at Wiley). Its characterizations are used as stated in Amenta, De Loera and Soberón (2017), Lemmas 2.6-2.7.
- **amenta2017**. Amenta, De Loera and Soberón (2017), in Algebraic and Geometric Methods in Discrete Mathematics, Contemp. Math. 685, AMS, pp. 55-95, DOI 10.1090/conm/685/13718. Read: arXiv 1508.07606v2. Theorem 2.2 (p. 2): pairwise intersecting boxes share a point; Lemma 2.7 (p. 4): Hoffman's characterization of h(S); Lemma 2.6 (p. 4) with the definition on p. 3: for discrete S, h(S) is the largest number of vertices of an S-vertex-polytope. Use: Proposition 3 credit, boxes, occupancy.
- **harborth1978**. Harborth (1978), Elemente der Mathematik 33(5):116-118 (no DOI; GDZ PPN378850199_0033). Read: page images. p. 116: for n >= 10 points with no three collinear there are always five forming a convex pentagon with no point inside; the nine-point figure shows ten is least. Use: order at least five for ten occupied positions.
- **horton1983**. Horton (1983), Canad. Math. Bull. 26(4):482-484, DOI 10.4153/CMB-1983-077-8. Read: Cambridge free PDF. p. 482: arbitrarily large sets with no empty convex 7-gon.
- **heule2024**. Heule and Scheucher (2024), TACAS 2024, LNCS 14570, pp. 61-80, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-57246-3_5. Read: arXiv 2403.00737v1 (the Springer PDF returned a challenge). Abstract and Theorem 1 (p. 2): every 30 points in general position contain an empty hexagon; Overmars's 29-point set has none. Use: order at least six for 30 occupied positions.
- **balogh2013**. Balogh, González-Aguilar and Salazar (2013), Comput. Geom. 46(6):725-733, DOI 10.1016/j.comgeo.2012.11.004. Read: arXiv 1206.0805v1. Abstract and Theorem 2: expected largest convex hole Θ(log n / log log n).
- **gardenfors2000**. Gärdenfors (2000), Conceptual Spaces, MIT Press, DOI 10.7551/mitpress/2076.001.0001, ISBN 0262071991. Identity only: the Internet Archive scan is lending-only and MIT Press Direct served a challenge. An OpenLibrary search-inside snippet shows the wording of Criterion P in section 3.5. Cited for the well-known proposal that natural properties are convex regions; the page was not inspected.
- **larose2007**. Larose, Loten and Tardif (2007), LMCS 3(4:6), pp. 1-22, DOI 10.2168/LMCS-3(4:6)2007. Read: published version. Theorem 2.5 (p. 6): first-order definability, finite duality, finite tree duality and bounded diameter of critical obstructions are equivalent, the first equivalence attributed to Atserias (2005); §2.2 (p. 5): definitions of obstructions and critical obstructions; abstract: decidability. The step from bounded obstruction order to finite duality in the manuscript is the paper's own argument.
- **greenberg1979**. Greenberg (1979), Econometrica 47(3):627-636, DOI 10.2307/1910410 (Crossref records page 627 only). Read: abstract (Econometric Society page); JSTOR served a challenge. The m/(m+1) threshold is from the abstract; nothing is claimed about the proof method.
- **aspvall1979**. Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan (1979), Inf. Process. Lett. 8(3):121-123, DOI 10.1016/0020-0190(79)90002-4. Identity only (closed). Cited for the standard criterion for satisfiability of two-literal clauses through strongly connected components of the implication graph.

## Maximum entropy and connected information

- **schneidman2003**. Schneidman, Still, Berry and Bialek (2003), PRL 91(23):238701, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.91.238701. Read: arXiv physics/0307072. Eq. (6), p. 2: connected information of order k; Eq. (5), p. 2: multi-information as the sum of connected informations; p. 1 and Fig. 1: the exclusive-or example.
- **schneidman2006**. Schneidman, Berry, Segev and Bialek (2006), Nature 440(7087):1007-1012, DOI 10.1038/nature04701. Read: PMC author manuscript (PMC1785327) and arXiv q-bio/0512013. Section "Are pairwise correlations enough?" and Fig. 2c-d: I(2)/I_N ≈ 90% in groups of ten salamander retinal ganglion cells.
- **amari2001**. Amari (2001), IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 47(5):1701-1711, DOI 10.1109/18.930911. Abstract only: orthogonal decomposition of dependency into pairwise, triplewise and higher-order parts.
- **darroch1972**. Darroch and Ratcliff (1972), Ann. Math. Statist. 43(5):1470-1480, DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177692379. Abstract only (Project Euclid blocked scripted access): iterative scaling for maximum-entropy distributions of product form under linear constraints. The implementation used here is iterative proportional fitting of two-way margins.

## Data and the trolley cases

- **awad2020**. Awad, Dsouza, Shariff, Rahwan and Bonnefon (2020), PNAS 117(5):2332-2337, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1911517117. Read: PMC full text (PMC7007553). Materials and Methods: scenario texts; three scenarios in random order; sample restricted to users answering all three. Results: in every country acceptance was highest for Switch, then Loop, then Footbridge (country-level averages 81%, 72%, 51%). Data Availability: OSF project mxa6z. The percentages reported in the manuscript are computed in the recorded run on the one-session-per-participant sample.
- **francken2022**. Francken et al. (2022), Neuroscience of Consciousness 2022(1):niac011, DOI 10.1093/nc/niac011. Read: PMC full text (PMC9374479). Methods: promoted mainly at ASSC 2018 and 2019, others could respond; 307 responses; the paper's analyses use 166 ASSC attendees doing consciousness research; five-point scales, with Q15 (transition) having three options. The OSF data show five options for Q15 in the 2019 online wave; the manuscript describes this. Data availability: OSF project xz3ua.
- **thomson1985**. Thomson (1985), Yale Law Journal 94(6):1395-1415, DOI 10.2307/796133 (Crossref gives the first page only; 1415 from the scan). Read: Yale Law School repository scan. p. 1395 credits Foot; p. 1402 the loop variant; p. 1409 the footbridge case ("Fat Man"); note 14 says several cases come from Thomson (1976), which was not read.
- **foot2002**. Foot, "The problem of abortion and the doctrine of the double effect" (1967), reprinted in Virtues and Vices (OUP 2002), pp. 19-32, DOI 10.1093/0199252866.003.0002. Identity only.
