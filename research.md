## Helly numbers and convexity

- [T1] Helly (1923), Jahresbericht DMV 32:175-176 (GDZ page images). p. 175: closed convex sets in n-dimensional space, every n+1 of which share a point, share a point. Supports the order d+1 of convex regions.
- [T1] Danzer, Grünbaum and Klee (1963), Proc. Symp. Pure Math. 7:101-180 (author-archive scan). p. 102: Helly's theorem; p. 124: the Helly number α and Motzkin's result for algebraic varieties (degree one gives flats, order n+1); p. 144: axis-parallel parallelotopes, γ₂ = 1, i.e. pairwise intersecting boxes share a point. Supports the definition of the Helly number and the order two of boxes.
- [T1] Averkov (2013), SIAM J. Discrete Math. 27(3) (arXiv v3). Definition 1.3 (S-convex sets) and 1.4 (Helly number); eq. (3.1): the Helly number of a family equals that of its finite intersections; Proposition 3.1: h(S) <= |S|, h(S ∩ C) <= h(S); Remark 4.5 and abstract: Doignon's h(Z^d) = 2^d. Supports Proposition 2's credit, the grid bound and the occupancy reading.
- [T2] Amenta, De Loera and Soberón (2017), Contemp. Math. 685 (arXiv v2). Lemma 2.7 (Hoffman's Proposition 2): h(S) is the largest size of R ⊆ S with the intersection of conv(R minus x_i) disjoint from S; Lemma 2.6: for discrete S, h(S) is the largest number of vertices of an S-vertex-polytope (points of S in convex position with no other point of S in their hull); Theorem 2.2: pairwise intersecting axis-parallel boxes share a point. Supports Proposition 3's credit and the empty-polygon characterization.
- [T1 identity; content via T2] Hoffman (1979), Ann. N.Y. Acad. Sci. 319:284-288; Doignon (1973), J. Geometry 3:71-85. Full texts not reachable; their results are cited as stated by Averkov and by Amenta, De Loera and Soberón.
- [T1] Harborth (1978), Elemente der Mathematik 33:116-118 (GDZ page images). p. 116: among n >= 10 points with no three collinear there are always five forming an empty convex pentagon; nine points do not suffice.
- [T1] Horton (1983), Canad. Math. Bull. 26(4):482-484. p. 482: arbitrarily large sets with no empty convex 7-gon.
- [T1] Heule and Scheucher (2024), TACAS 2024, LNCS 14570:61-80 (arXiv 2403.00737 v1). Abstract and Theorem 1: every 30-point set in general position contains an empty hexagon; a 29-point set without one is due to Overmars.
- [T1] Balogh, González-Aguilar and Salazar (2013), Comput. Geom. 46(6):725-733 (arXiv v1). Abstract and Theorem 2: the expected largest convex hole of n uniform random points in a bounded convex region has Θ(log n / log log n) vertices.
- [T1 identity; wording via search snippet] Gärdenfors (2000), Conceptual Spaces, MIT Press. Criterion P, that a natural property is a convex region of a domain, is in §3.5; the page was not inspected. Cited for this well-known proposal only.
- [T1] Larose, Loten and Tardif (2007), LMCS 3(4:6). Theorem 2.5 (p. 6): first-order definability of the CSP, finite duality, a finite complete set of tree obstructions and bounded diameter of critical obstructions are equivalent, the first equivalence due to Atserias; §2.2 (p. 5) defines obstructions and critical obstructions; the abstract and Theorems 6.1-6.2: deciding first-order definability is NP-complete in general and polynomial for cores.
- [T1 abstract] Greenberg (1979), Econometrica 47(3):627-636. Abstract: for compact convex sets of alternatives of dimension m, a d-majority equilibrium exists for every profile of convex continuous preferences iff d > (m/(m+1))n. The proof method was not inspected.

## Logic, constraint satisfaction and aggregation

- [T1] Baker and Pixley (1975), Math. Z. 143(2):165-174 (GDZ scan, OCR checked against the page image by the verification pass). Theorem 2.1, p. 167: a variety has a (d+1)-variable near-unanimity term iff every subalgebra of a finite direct product is determined by its d-fold coordinate projections (their d is the k of the paper). Supports Proposition 4's first part and Proposition 5.
- [T1] Feder and Vardi (1998), SIAM J. Comput. 28(1):57-104 (authors' full preprint, pages and numbering of the preprint). Section 6.1 (pp. 22-24): bounded width via Datalog; Section 6.1.2 (pp. 28-29): strict width, the l-Helly property (if fixing some values leaves an instance unsolvable, some l of them already do) and the l-mapping property (an (l+1)-ary near-unanimity polymorphism); Theorem 24: the three are equivalent and decidable. Section 6.2 and Theorem 30 (p. 34): problems with the ability to count, including linear equations over Abelian groups, do not have bounded width. The Helly-type reading of near-unanimity is theirs.
- [T1 abstract] Jeavons, Cohen and Cooper (1998), Artif. Intell. 101(1-2):251-265. Abstract (archived CiteSeerX summary of the preprint): an algebraic property characterizes the constraint types for which strong k-consistency ensures global consistency. Theorem statement not inspected.
- [T1 abstract] Barto and Kozik (2014), J. ACM 61(1), article 3. Abstract: problems without the ability to count are solvable by local consistency, settling the Feder-Vardi conjecture.
- [T1 identity; content via T2] Schaefer (1978), STOC 1978, pp. 216-226 (ACM DL challenge). Chen (2006), SIGACT News 37(4):85-114 (arXiv cs/0611018), Theorem 3.20 (p. 12): Schaefer's theorem in algebraic form (tractable iff a constant, AND, OR, majority or minority polymorphism); pp. 3-6: AND preserves Horn clauses, majority preserves two-literal clauses, minority preserves linear equations.
- [T1] Beeri, Fagin, Maier and Yannakakis (1983), J. ACM 30(3):479-513 (scan of the published version from Fagin's archived author page). p. 485: pairwise and global consistency; Theorem 3.4 (p. 488): acyclicity is equivalent to "every pairwise consistent database over R is globally consistent" among twelve conditions.
- [T1 abstract] Vorob'ev (1962), Theory Probab. Appl. 7(2):147-163. Abstract (Crossref): a family of measures is consistent if every pair is; regularity of the complex is necessary and sufficient for extendability of every consistent family.
- [T1] Abramsky and Brandenburger (2011), New J. Phys. 13:113036 (open). Section 2.5 (p. 9): empirical models as compatible families; Section 3 (pp. 11-12): non-locality and contextuality as the non-existence of global sections; Theorem 8.1 (p. 28).
- [T1] Reiter (1987), Artif. Intell. 32(1):57-95 (scan at cs.ru.nl). Definition 2.4 (p. 63), Definitions 4.1-4.3 and Theorem 4.4 (p. 67): diagnoses are the minimal hitting sets of the conflict sets.
- [T1 abstract; definitions via T2] Hansson (1994), J. Symb. Logic 59(3):845-859. Abstract: kernel contraction generalizes safe contraction and is axiomatically characterized. Definitions of kernels and incision functions read in Hansson's own SEP entry "Logic of Belief Revision", Section 5.2, not in the 1994 paper.
- [T1] Liffiton and Sakallah (2008), J. Autom. Reasoning 40(1):1-33 (author preprint). Definition 3 (p. 6) and Theorem 1 (p. 7): MCSes and MUSes are irreducible hitting sets of each other; the proof is attributed to Birnbaum and Lozinskii (2003).
- [T1] Dung (1995), Artif. Intell. 77(2):321-357 (course-site copy of the published version). Definitions 2 and 5 (p. 326): argumentation frameworks with a binary attack relation; conflict-free sets.
- [T1] Nielsen and Parsons (2007), ArgMAS 2006, LNCS, pp. 54-73 (archived author copy). Abstract and Definition 1 (p. 6): attacks by nonempty sets of arguments; Dung's framework is the case of singleton attackers.
- [T1] List and Pettit (2002), Econ. Philos. 18(1):89-110 (LSE copy of the published version). p. 96: the discursive dilemma; Theorem 1 (p. 100): impossibility of anonymous, systematic aggregation into complete, consistent and deductively closed judgment sets.
- [T1 identity; content via the authors' Part II] Nehring and Puppe (2007), J. Econ. Theory 135(1):269-305. Their Part II working paper (revised March 2005, p. 30): "By NP, Proposition 4.1, median spaces are characterized by the property that all critical families have cardinality two", with issue-by-issue majority voting consistent on median spaces. Proposition numbering in the published Part I not verified.
- [T1] Dietrich and List (2007), J. Theor. Polit. 19(4):391-424 (LSE accepted manuscript). Section 2.1 (p. 6): minimal inconsistent sets; Corollary 2(a) (p. 12): a uniform quota rule is consistent iff m > n - n/z; Section 3.3 (p. 13): preference cycles of length k give z = k and the Craven and Ferejohn-Grether thresholds.
- [T1 identity] Rescher (2009), Aporetics, University of Pittsburgh Press. No accessible text; cited for the notion of aporetic clusters only.
- [T1] Lee (2009), Electron. J. Combin. 16(1), N3 (open). Proposition 1 (p. 2): minimal unsatisfiable 2-SAT formulas have at most 4n - 2 clauses (implication-path argument); abstract: minimal unsatisfiable k-SAT formulas with Ω(n^k) clauses for k >= 3.
- [T1] Abbasizanjani and Kullmann, arXiv 2003.03639v3 (2026). p. 4: nonsingular minimally unsatisfiable 2-CNFs are the cycles of equivalences B_n with 2n clauses; singular DP-reduction preserves deficiency and reduces 2-MUs of deficiency k >= 2 to B_k. The bound of 2n clauses is inferred from these statements; p. 10 contains a conflicting numeral that is not used.
- [T1 identity] Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan (1979). Standard strongly connected component criterion for two-literal clauses.

## Maps of philosophy

- [T1] Bourget and Chalmers (2014), Phil. Studies 170(3):465-500 (author preprint of 27 April 2013). Preprint pp. 4, 10: 1,972 faculty in 99 departments, 931 responded; p. 5: thirty main questions; p. 12: "Contingency tables such as Table 3 are available for all answers pairs on the survey site"; p. 13 and Table 4: correlations between views; p. 19: exploratory factor and principal component analyses.
- [T1 abstract] Bourget and Chalmers (2023), Philosophers' Imprint 23. Abstract: around 2000 philosophers surveyed on 100 questions; correlations and demographic effects reported. Full text not reachable (bot challenge at the publisher and at PhilArchive).
- [T1] Noichl (2021), Synthese 198(6):5089-5100 (PhilSci-Archive manuscript). 68,152 records; bibliographic coupling vectors compared by cosine similarity, reduced with truncated SVD and UMAP to a planar map, clustered with HDBSCAN; nouns used only to label clusters.
- [T1 abstract] Malaterre, Chartier and Pulizzotto (2019), HOPOS 9(2):215-249. Topic modeling of the full-text corpus of Philosophy of Science, 1934-2015, 126 topics.
- [T1] Weatherson (2022), A History of Philosophy Journals, Volume 1, Maize Books (open EPUB). Introduction: a 90-topic LDA model of JSTOR word counts for English-language research articles of twelve journals, 1876-2013.
- [T1] Shi and Haupt (2026), FAccT '26, pp. 6280-6304 (arXiv 2604.23575 v2 read). Abstract: 277 PhilPeople profiles; validation against the full 2020 survey, N = 1,785; language models over-correlate philosophical judgments. §3.1 (p. 3): the 2020 survey "reports aggregate distributions but does not release individual-level responses linked to demographic profiles". Repository README: individual-level data on request.

## Maximum entropy and connected information

- [T1] Schneidman, Still, Berry and Bialek (2003), PRL 91:238701 (arXiv physics/0307072). Eq. (6): connected information of order k as the entropy difference of maximum-entropy distributions consistent with marginals of order k-1 and k; Eq. (5): the multi-information is the sum of connected informations; p. 1 and Fig. 1: exclusive or of two random bits has zero pairwise information and one bit of multi-information, all of third order.
- [T1] Schneidman, Berry, Segev and Bialek (2006), Nature 440:1007-1012 (PMC author manuscript). The pairwise maximum-entropy model captures I(2)/I_N ≈ 90% of the multi-information in groups of ten retinal ganglion cells.
- [T1 abstract] Amari (2001), IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 47(5):1701-1711. Orthogonal decomposition of stochastic dependency into pairwise, triplewise and higher-order parts.
- [T1 abstract] Darroch and Ratcliff (1972), Ann. Math. Statist. 43(5):1470-1480. Iterative scaling for distributions of product form obtained by maximizing entropy subject to linear constraints.

## Data and the trolley cases

- [T1] Awad, Dsouza, Shariff, Rahwan and Bonnefon (2020), PNAS 117(5):2332-2337 (PMC full text). Materials and Methods: the classic mode presented Switch, Loop and Footbridge in random order; the authors restricted their sample to users who answered all three; Results: every country endorsed sacrifice most for Switch, then Loop, then Footbridge; Data Availability: OSF.
- [T1] Francken et al. (2022), Neuroscience of Consciousness 2022(1):niac011 (PMC full text). Methods: promoted mainly at ASSC 2018 and 2019, others could respond; 307 responses; analyses restricted to ASSC attendees doing consciousness research; five-point Likert questions, Q15 with three options; Data availability: OSF xz3ua. The OSF file shows that the transition question had five options in the 2019 online wave.
- [T1] Thomson (1985), Yale Law Journal 94(6):1395-1415 (Yale repository scan). p. 1402: the loop variant; p. 1409: the footbridge case ("Fat Man"); p. 1395: credits Foot with the original problem.
- [T1 identity] Foot (1967/2002), "The problem of abortion and the doctrine of the double effect", reprinted in Virtues and Vices (OUP 2002), pp. 19-32. Cited for the origin of the trolley case.
